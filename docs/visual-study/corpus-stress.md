# Corpus: stress cases (6)

Pass A. Written to find where the envelope or an idiom breaks.

### S-1 · no participants
> "Everything has felt far away lately."

| # | Statement | Cat. | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | For a while, everything has felt far away to own | R | a feeling, attributed |
| I1 | Own feels lonely or low | I | |

**Representation**
- Elements: `own` presence, own
- Statements: `s1` feels(own, distance from everything) R, lasting
- Qualities: openness? weight? Neither says "far away"
- Facets: field (a quality of distance around own)

**Gaps:** a felt relation (far from everything) is not a spatial one, and v0 can only say it as a quality, which loses it (G-22). There are no participants and no relations, which the envelope allows: only the field idiom has anything to draw.

### S-2 · mostly quantity
> "I slept four hours a night this week, against my usual seven."

| # | Statement | Cat. | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Own slept four hours a night this week | R | |
| R2 | Own usually sleeps seven | R | |

**Representation**
- Elements: `own` presence, own · `sleep` quantity
- Statements: `s1` value(sleep, 4 h/night, this week) R · `s2` value(sleep, 7 h/night, usual) R
- Facets: quantities

**Gaps:** none in v0. But the right figure is a plain comparison, and that is a chart's job, not the engine's (G-23).

### S-3 · negation and a counterfactual
> "If I hadn't said anything, she wouldn't have left early."

| # | Statement | Cat. | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | Own said something | L | presupposition of the counterfactual |
| L2 | She left early | L | presupposition |
| R1 | Own holds that what they said is why she left early | R | the writer's own conclusion, attributed |
| I1 | It is true that she left because of it | I | |
| U1 | Why she left | U | |

**Representation**
- Elements: `own` presence, own · `other` presence
- Statements: `s1` sends(own, words, other) L · `s2` leaves(other, early) L · `s3` believes(own, causes(s1, s2)) R · `q1` question(s2, cause) U
- Facets: dynamics

**Gaps:** counterfactuals (G-24); attributed belief (G-19); causation (G-17). Drawing `s3` as a cause would draw the person's self-blame as fact. It belongs in a register for "the writer's own view", which v0 doesn't have.

### S-4 · a long account
> "On Monday my presentation got moved, on Tuesday I missed the bus and was late, on Wednesday my landlord emailed about the rent, and by Thursday I just felt behind on everything."

| # | Statement | Cat. | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Monday: own's presentation was moved | R | |
| R2 | Tuesday: own missed the bus and was late | R | |
| R3 | Wednesday: own's landlord emailed about the rent | R | |
| R4 | By Thursday own felt behind on everything | R | attributed |
| L1 | The writer puts R1–R3 in one sequence leading to R4 | L | "and by Thursday" |
| I1 | R1–R3 caused R4 | I | |

**Representation**
- Elements: `own` presence, own · four events (process) · `landlord` presence, secondary
- Statements: `s1`–`s3` the three events R, one per phase · `s4` feels(own, weight 0.7) R, Thursday · `s5` precedes(s1–s3, s4) L
- Time: four phases
- Facets: dynamics

**Gaps:** v0 can say it, but an idiom drawing every event passes the element budget (`visual-policy.md` §3.13, about seven forms). Events need to be groupable (G-25).

### S-5 · two words
> "Exhausted again."

| # | Statement | Cat. | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Own feels exhausted | R | |
| R2 | It has happened before | R | "again" |

**Representation**
- Elements: `own` presence, own
- Statements: `s1` feels(own, weight 0.8) R · `s2` recurs(s1) R
- Facets: field (weight)

**Gaps:** recurrence (G-10). There is very little to show, and an idiom that invents a setting would assert one. "No figure" may be the right answer (investigation §6, Q5).

### S-6 · a self-contradiction
> "I don't care what they think. It's just that I keep wondering what they think."

| # | Statement | Cat. | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Own says they don't care what others think | R | attributed |
| R2 | Own keeps wondering what others think | R | attributed |
| L1 | The writer sets R2 against R1 | L | "It's just that" |

**Representation**
- Elements: `own` presence, own · `others` presence, plural
- Statements: `s1` feels(own, indifference to others' view) R · `s2` recurs(attends(own, others' view)) R
- Facets: field

**Gaps:** two reported statements in tension, with nothing to carry the tension (G-26); attending to something (G-10). An idiom must show both and resolve neither.
