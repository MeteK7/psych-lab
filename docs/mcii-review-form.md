## Before you rate: try the exercise
Go through the exercise once in the app, with a **made-up wish**. Please don't use a real painful experience. Nothing you type is saved. The session lead may enter your answers for you on a video call.

If you have time for a second run, it helps a lot. Choose differently: use "It's mostly something outside me", then **Set it aside**.

## How to rate
For **every** line below:
- **Plausible or clear, 1 to 5:**
  - **Cards:** "Could an ordinary person realistically think, feel or do this?"
  - **Other texts:** "Is this clear, and would it make sense to an ordinary person at this point?"
  - 1 = not at all, 3 = somewhat, 5 = very
- **Could it feel blaming or discouraging?** ☐ No ☐ Yes. If yes, say why in a few words. Examples:
  - it suggests something is wrong with the person
  - it makes the wish sound unrealistic, or giving up sound like failure
  - it pushes the person to keep going, or to stop
  - it's falsely cheerful or preachy
- **Suggested rewrite** (optional).

Please also flag anything that touches on clinical topics, trauma, health or crisis, or that might mean different things in different cultures.

## For the session lead: decision rule
The procedure, reviewers, consolidation, safety veto and round 2 are the same as `cognitive-shifts-review-plan.md` §2–5, with the changes below. Ratings go into `mcii-review-results.xlsx`, which has one row per item above, in the same order and with the same wording. If this form's texts change, the spreadsheet must change with it.

- **Walkthrough:** http://localhost:4310/areas/action-growth, one run at least, in the development build. Note any confusion, but don't explain the texts.
- **An item stays** if its mean rating across reviewers is **≥ 4** and no reviewer's concern remains unresolved after consolidation. A concern is resolved when at least two of the three agree it doesn't apply, or when the item is rewritten and passes round 2.
- **Safety veto:** a blame, discouragement or safety concern raised by a reviewer with psychology or counselling training is resolved **only** if the item is rewritten and re-rated in round 2, or dropped. Discussion or a majority alone never resolves it.
- **Steering (part I, question 1):** any answer that the exercise pushes people to keep going or to drop a wish is discussed in consolidation, even if every item passed. The design promises never to do this.
- **The picture (part H):** if two or more reviewers say the wall makes the wish seem blocked, the bridge looks already solved, or the faded path looks like failure, the picture is redrawn. If two or more say it gets in the way, it can be removed, because the exercise works without it.
- **Learning check (part I, question 8):** answers should come close to "look at what you want and what's in the way, then decide, and plan for the obstacle". If they don't, this is discussed in consolidation even if every item passed.
- **Overall concerns:** if two or more reviewers raise the same one, it's treated as a failed item.
- **Minimum content:** at least 3 obstacle cards and 3 plan cards. Falling below this means redesigning that step, not releasing it thinner.
- **Failing items** are rewritten and re-rated in round 2, or dropped. An item that fails round 2 is dropped.
- **Changes after the review** get a new content version (`mcii.v3.json`), because practice runs of the earlier versions exist in `app_dev`. Those files stay unchanged.
- **Recording:** the outcome is entered in `content-review.md` (review record) with date, reviewer codes and changes.

**Scope:** this form covers what's on screen during the exercise. The science explanation is checked separately, using the criteria in `content-review.md`.

**This is one of three gates.** Release also needs usability sessions and the protocol change (`mcii-design.md` §7).

## Reviewer briefing (to print)

> **Thank you for reviewing.** This is a short exercise on a website. People choose an everyday wish, picture the best part of it, name what's likely to get in the way, decide what to do with the wish, and can make a plan. It isn't therapy, and it's only for everyday wishes such as learning, work or friends.
>
> **What we need from you:**
> - Does each line sound like something an ordinary person could think, feel or do, and is it clear?
> - Could any line feel blaming or discouraging to someone reading it about their own wish, or push them to keep going or to stop?
>
> **How:**
> 1. First, try the exercise once using a made-up wish, either at my computer or on a video call where I share my screen and enter what you tell me. Nothing typed is saved.
> 2. Then fill in the form on your own. Rate every line from 1 to 5, and tick "blaming or discouraging" if it could feel that way, with a few words on why.
> 3. Rewrites are welcome but optional.
>
> **Please don't:** discuss your ratings with the other reviewers before handing in the form, or use a real painful experience in the walkthrough.
>
> **Afterwards:** I go through only the lines that got low ratings or concerns, either in a short video call or by sending you a list with proposed decisions, to which you reply agree, disagree or with a rewrite. Your form is identified by a code, not your name. It's kept only until the results are recorded, then destroyed.
