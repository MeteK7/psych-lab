# Change the camera angle: content review form

> **For:** reviewers of "Change the camera angle" (`camera-angle-design.md`; the texts as built are in §10 and `web/src/content/cs-camera-angle.v1.json`).
> **At least 3 reviewers,** including one with psychology or counselling training, who holds a safety veto and a veto on the figures (`visual-policy.md` §7). Each reviewer fills in a copy independently, without seeing the others' answers.
> **Time:** about 35 minutes, after a 10-minute walkthrough.

## Before you rate: try the exercise
Go through the exercise twice in the app, with a **made-up, everyday moment**, such as "a colleague cut me off in a meeting". Please don't use a real painful experience. Nothing you type is saved. The session lead may enter your answers for you on a video call.

- **Run 1:** write something at step 2. At step 3, step back to "From across the room". At step 5, answer "Yes" or "Partly" to "Can you do something about the situation itself?"
- **Run 2:** press "Skip this step" at step 2. At step 3, stay at "Through your own eyes". At step 5, answer "No".

Step 4 and the closing differ between the two runs, so both are needed.

## How to rate
For **every** line below:
- **Plausible or clear, 1 to 5:**
  - **Options:** "Could an ordinary person realistically notice or think this?"
  - **Other texts:** "Is this clear, and would it make sense to an ordinary person at this point?"
  - 1 = not at all, 3 = somewhat, 5 = very
- **Could it feel invalidating or pushy?** ☐ No ☐ Yes. If yes, say why in a few words. Examples:
  - it suggests the moment matters less, or that the person overreacted
  - it suggests that stepping further back is the right answer, or that staying close is a lesser one
  - it suggests the person should feel better afterwards
  - it's falsely cheerful, preachy or therapeutic
- **Suggested rewrite** (optional).

Please also flag anything that touches on clinical topics, trauma, health or crisis, or that might mean different things in different cultures.

## For the session lead: decision rule
The procedure, reviewers, consolidation, safety veto and round 2 are the same as `cognitive-shifts-review-plan.md` §2–5, with the changes below.

- **Walkthrough:** http://localhost:4310/areas/cognitive-shifts, both runs, in the development build. Note any confusion, but don't explain the texts.
- **An item stays** if its mean rating across reviewers is **≥ 4** and no reviewer's concern remains unresolved after consolidation. A concern is resolved when at least two of the three agree it doesn't apply, or when the item is rewritten and passes round 2.
- **Safety veto:** an invalidation or safety concern raised by a reviewer with psychology or counselling training is resolved **only** if the item is rewritten and re-rated in round 2, or dropped. Discussion or a majority alone never resolves it.
- **The figures (part H, question 1):** a "no" from the reviewer with psychology training means the figures are redrawn before release (`visual-policy.md` §7).
- **Direction (part I, questions 1 and 2, and part H, question 2):** any answer that the exercise suggests feeling better, stepping further back, or that the moment matters less is discussed in consolidation, even if every item passed. The design promises never to do this (`camera-angle-design.md` §9.9).
- **The picture (part H):**
  - If two or more reviewers say the room gets in the way, the decision to show it at step 4 is reopened. Step 3 can't work without it.
  - If two or more say the other rooms distract, position 3 is reconsidered.
- **Learning check (part I, question 7):** answers should come close to "look at the moment again, then from further back if you like, and see what you notice". If they don't, this is discussed in consolidation even if every item passed.
- **Overall concerns:** if two or more reviewers raise the same one, it's treated as a failed item.
- **Minimum content:** at least 4 options besides "Nothing looks different from here", in both versions of step 4. Falling below this means redesigning that step, not releasing it thinner.
- **Failing items** are rewritten and re-rated in round 2, or dropped. An item that fails round 2 is dropped.
- **Changes after the review** get a new content version (`cs-camera-angle.v2.json`), because practice runs of v1 exist in `app_dev`. That file stays unchanged.
- **Recording:** the outcome is entered in `content-review.md` (review record) with date, reviewer codes and changes. The psychology-trained reviewer's decisions on `camera-angle-design.md` §9 are recorded there too.

**Scope:** this form covers what's on screen during the exercise, and the picture. The science explanation is checked separately, using the criteria in `content-review.md`.

**This is one of the release gates** (`camera-angle-design.md` §7). Release also needs the full texts of the remaining sources, your R3 and the psychology-trained reviewer in the test environment, and the "safe unsupervised" rating.

## Reviewer briefing (to print)

> **Thank you for reviewing.** This is a short exercise on a website. People pick an everyday moment that still bothers them a little and go back to it briefly through their own eyes, or skip that. Then they can step back through a simple drawn room, as far as feels right, and look at the moment from there. It isn't therapy, and it's only for everyday moments, such as at work, in learning or with friends.
>
> **What we need from you:**
> - Is each line clear, and does each option sound like something an ordinary person could notice or think?
> - Could any line feel invalidating to someone reading it about their own moment? For example, suggesting the moment matters less, that they should feel better, or that stepping further back is the right answer.
> - Do the drawn people read as people, without suggesting anyone in particular or how anyone feels?
>
> **How:**
> 1. First, try the exercise twice using a made-up moment, either at my computer or on a video call where I share my screen and enter what you tell me. Nothing typed is saved.
> 2. Then fill in the form on your own. Rate every line from 1 to 5, and tick "invalidating or pushy" if it could feel that way, with a few words on why.
> 3. Rewrites are welcome but optional.
>
> **Please don't:** discuss your ratings with the other reviewers before handing in the form, or use a real painful experience in the walkthrough.
>
> **Afterwards:** I go through only the lines that got low ratings or concerns, either in a short video call or by sending you a list with proposed decisions, to which you reply agree, disagree or with a rewrite. Your form is identified by a code, not your name. It's kept only until the results are recorded, then destroyed.
