# Consent form: trying out a set of exercises (usability test)

> **Status:** draft, 2026-11-01, for your review. Print two copies per tester: one to keep, one to sign. Procedure: `usability-procedure.md`.

## What this is about
I'm building a small set of interactive exercises on a computer. They're meant to help people get started on learning something, for example by planning a first step, or by following a moving dot while holding a picture in mind. I'd like to see whether they're **easy to understand and use**, and how they feel to someone trying them for the first time.

**This is not a test of you,** and not a medical or psychological study. The exercises haven't been shown to work, and this session doesn't try to find that out.

## What you'd do
- Try four short exercises on this computer, one after another (about 30 minutes in total).
- After each one, answer a few short questions. At the end, answer a short questionnaire. The whole session takes about an hour, with a break if you like.
- You'll be asked to think of something you'd like to start learning. **A made-up goal is fine.**
- One exercise asks you to picture something you've been putting off. Choose an everyday situation, **never a painful personal memory.**

## Your choices
- Taking part is **voluntary**.
- You can **stop at any time**, skip any question, or leave any exercise, without giving a reason.
- You can **withdraw** within 30 days of the session. Your data is then deleted straight away.

## What is recorded, and for how long
- **The exercises:** what you enter and your answers inside them, stored on this computer only, under a code such as `T7Q`, never your name. The picture you describe in the picture exercise is **never stored**.
- **Question sheets:** your answers and my notes, under the same code.
- **Your name** appears only on this form, which is kept separately from the code.
- **Deletion:**
  - any free-text answers are deleted once the summary is written
  - **all your data is deleted within 30 days**
  - this form is kept only as long as needed to show you agreed, then destroyed
- **No health questions** are asked, apart from whether any exercise made you feel worse.
- **Nothing is shared** with anyone else. A summary may describe results without names or codes.
